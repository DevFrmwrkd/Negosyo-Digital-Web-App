About the studio

A clinic built around the anxious patient

Silahis Dental Studio is a family and cosmetic dental clinic in Kapitolyo, Pasig offering preventive care, restorations, orthodontics, and implants. It's for families, professionals, and especially patients who have put off the dentist for years.

The studio was founded on a simple observation: most people don't fear dentistry — they fear being rushed, surprised, and billed without explanation. So every visit here starts with a conversation, every treatment plan is written down, and no procedure begins until you understand it.

Our dentists pursue continuing education yearly, and the clinic runs on digital X-rays, autoclave-sterilized instruments per patient, and appointment blocks long enough that no one is treated by the clock.

Common questions

Before your visit

How do I book an appointment?

Book through Messenger, by phone, or walk in — Messenger is fastest and we confirm your slot within the hour during clinic hours. First visits include a full exam and treatment plan discussion.

Do you accept HMO cards?

Yes, we are accredited with major Philippine HMOs. Message us your card details before your visit and we verify coverage so there are no surprises at the desk.

Is teeth cleaning painful?

Routine cleaning is generally comfortable — most patients feel light pressure and vibration, not pain. For sensitive teeth we adjust technique and can apply topical numbing.

Do you treat children?

Yes, we see patients from age three. First pediatric visits are kept short and gentle — a ride in the chair, a count of the teeth, and fluoride varnish if the child is ready.

What should I bring to a first visit?

A valid ID, your HMO card if you have one, and any recent panoramic X-ray. If you have none, we can take digital X-rays in the clinic.

Do you handle dental emergencies?

Yes — for severe toothache, knocked-out teeth, or broken restorations, call the clinic and we will fit you into the same day's schedule whenever possible.

How often should I have a check-up?

Every six months for most adults and children. Patients with gum disease or braces may need shorter intervals, which your dentist will set with you.
