Comforts Everything that is actually here

The short list, honestly kept.

14-metre lagoon pool

Salt-filtered, unheated, lit until ten. Deep end 1.6 m, with a shallow ledge for small children and one shaded bench nobody fights over.

Aircon in every room

Split-type inverters, serviced quarterly, plus ceiling fans if you would rather sleep with the doors open and the screens down.

Fibre wifi throughout

One line into the house and a mesh point in each casita. Fine for calls from the balcony; we will show you the corner that is fastest.

A kitchen you may use

Gas hob, oven, a big fridge and a chest freezer for whatever comes back from the market. Label what is yours and it will still be there.

Breakfast on the terrace

Rice, eggs, fruit, longganisa or danggit, and coffee from Bohol beans — served seven to ten under the mango tree.

Parking inside the gate

Room for three cars and a van behind the wall, gate shut overnight. Motorbikes go under cover at the side of the balay.

Laundry, same day

Washed, dried and folded by late afternoon if it is in the basket before nine. Priced by the kilo at the front table.

Hot water, always

Solar tanks with an electric backup, so the last shower of the evening is as good as the first one of the morning.

The place About Villa Marilag

A family house that learned to keep guests.

Villa Marilag was built in 1994 as one family's holiday house on the Danao side of Panglao, back when the road ran out in sand and the nearest runway was in Tagbilaran. It stayed that way for twenty years — full at Christmas, quiet the rest of the year.

In 2019 we opened four of the rooms, then added the two casitas in the garden, and left everything else exactly where it was: the same mango tree, the same cement tiles, the same long pool the family dug in 1998 because the sea was a ten-minute walk and the children were small.

There is no front desk in the hotel sense. There is a table by the door where somebody is usually sitting, a kitchen you are welcome in, and an understanding that the pool belongs to whoever is awake first. Six keys is as big as we intend to get.

“We would rather be full six rooms at a time than build a seventh.”

Ador & Nenita Villalon — owners

Good to know House rules & policies

House rules, in plain words.

0917 555 0186
What time is check-in and check-out?

Check-in is from 2:00 pm and check-out is 11:00 am. If your flight lands early we will hold your bags and you are welcome to swim while the room is turned over — most mornings it is ready before noon anyway. Late check-out to 2:00 pm is ₱800 when the room is not booked that night, and free when nobody is waiting on it. Tell us your arrival time when you book and somebody will be at the gate.

Are children and extra guests allowed?

Children of any age are welcome and there is no charge for under-threes sharing a bed. Past the listed occupancy we add a rollaway at ₱650 a night including breakfast, up to one extra person per room. The pool has a shallow ledge but no lifeguard, so children swim with an adult — that one is not negotiable.

Can we bring a pet?

Small dogs are allowed in the two garden casitas by arrangement, not in the main balay and not in the pool. There is no pet fee, but we ask for a ₱2,000 refundable deposit against damage and that they are not left alone in the casita. If another guest has declared an allergy for the same dates we will tell you before you pay anything.

What about parties, functions and day visitors?

This is a six-room house with families in it, so quiet hours run 10:00 pm to 7:00 am and we do not host events, functions or day visitors who are not staying. A whole-villa booking is different: with the gate closed and no other guests, the garden and the pool are yours as late as you like, provided the neighbours can still sleep.

How do we pay, and what happens if we cancel?

A 30% deposit confirms the booking, by bank transfer or GCash, with the balance in cash or transfer on arrival. Cancel more than 14 days out and the deposit comes back in full; inside 14 days it is held as credit for a year; inside 48 hours it is retained. Typhoons and cancelled flights are handled as credit rather than a forfeit — we have been on the wrong side of that ourselves.

Is there smoking, and where?

No smoking or vaping indoors, in the casitas, or on the upper balcony. There is an ashtray at the far end of the pool deck by the wall, which is where everybody ends up talking at eleven anyway. A room that has been smoked in costs ₱3,000 to air and re-wash, which we would very much rather not charge anybody.
